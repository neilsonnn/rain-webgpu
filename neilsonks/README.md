The neilsonks.com site in the `site` repository. Modernized from `/Users/neilson/Projects/Website/neilsonks` using React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Phosphor icons, and Zustand.

## Run

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

`npm run build` typechecks, builds into `dist/` with static entry files for the active page URLs, and verifies the About sentence, media paths, and exclusion of the writing drafts. `npm run preview` serves that production output locally.

Its **Earlier / Current** switch and **Arrangement** selector preserve the archived compositions without changing tabs. The local workbench's **Save as defaults** also uses the development-only Vite endpoint for `src/glass/default-presets.json`. These source presets ship in future builds. **Reset study** (or **Match reference** in Current) restores saved defaults; workbench slider autosave remains independent. Public debug changes apply live and become defaults only when explicitly saved. **Wire thickness** controls real mesh diameter. **Glass bump size** controls texture scale separately from height and micro-roughness, consistently across both interfaces, slopes, reflection, and refraction. The **Current** workbench renderer has sparse leaves at upper left, a yellow-green patch below, and a fuller bush on the right. The original reference photograph remains a comparison thumbnail only in the local workbench. It is never used as the Three.js background or a material texture. No dependencies were added.

The working construction is square wire mesh embedded in 6 mm obscure glass, following the user's identified [Architectural Glass Obscure Wire 6mm example](https://architecturalglass.com/products/wire-glass). The mesh is enabled by default; additional raised glass grid relief defaults to zero. The irregular, pebbled glass surface covers the entire pane and refracts the embedded strands as well as the plants. Its analytic gradient drives both dielectric reflection and refraction, and rays refine their entry against its physical height at oblique angles. Front and rear faces have independently generated RGBA16F height/normal fields at physical scale, with alpha storing surface height. Half-float slopes avoid 8-bit stepping in sharp reflections. IOR 1.52, wire gauge, pitch, and metal finish are tunable modeling assumptions; the product page does not specify all of these properties or establish the exact product in the reference photograph.

**Wired patterned glass** restores mesh visibility and zero surface-grid relief without resetting the user's optical, lighting, or grading adjustments. Optional glass-grid relief retains the separate embossed construction for experiments: rounded crests, smooth crossings, and cell pebbling form one continuous height field. Crest height, width, and pebbling remain adjustable in their own control group.

Front micro-roughness is independent of rear transmission scattering. The front uses its own GGX roughness for reflection and front-interface sampling, while the rear interface retains the scattering control. The softer reference setting lowers normal relief from 1 to .72, broadens front micro-roughness from .055 to .105, and lowers reflected-light intensity. The 512px HDR cube capture places the viewer inside a shaded enclosure with narrow horizontal and vertical light openings, mullions, roof beams, and an atmospheric sky. This architecture supplies reflections without appearing as props behind the plants. The dark frontal field preserves black gaps between bright facet reflections. Highlights result from surface normals, dielectric Fresnel response, and lighting. **Glass reflections**, **Sky fill**, and **Recess brightness** adjust reflected light, ambient illumination, and the shaded backdrop independently.

Transmission samples 24 paths through the two interfaces, with Snell refraction, exact unpolarized dielectric Fresnel terms, Smith masking, Beer–Lambert attenuation over the distance **inside the glass**, and a prefiltered first internal reflection. Surface slopes use [Heitz's GGX visible-normal sampling](https://jcgt.org/published/0007/04/01/paper.pdf), based on the rough-interface transmission approach in [Walter et al.](https://www.graphics.cornell.edu/~bjw/microfacetbsdf.pdf). Each transmitted path refines its intersection against garden depth. A 1.5× wider source frustum provides scene data outside the visible edges; finite-solid-angle mip filtering smooths distant samples. The entire optical composite remains linear half-float HDR until the final display transform.

The embedded steel mesh consists of actual instanced cylindrical strands with twenty-sided cross-sections, independently phased centreline irregularities, and intersecting front/rear families at welded joints. The reference diameter is 1.4 mm, increased from .76 mm for more visible weight. Diameter, embed depth, roughness, and oxidation are editable. Embed depth keeps both families within the slab's relief envelope. Carbon steel is approximated with [Johnson and Christy's measured iron optical constants](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.9.5056), interpolated at 640, 560, and 440 nm. The conductor's normal-incidence reflectance accounts for immersion in glass; GGX lighting, a rougher steel finish, subtle brown oxide, environment reflections, and geometric shadows complete the material. Exact alloy/coating properties remain unmeasured. Separate multisampled color/depth buffers preserve the strands' location inside the pane; front-interface rays sample that layer before exiting through the rear face. Its rendering pass is skipped when the mesh is hidden.

Diffusion controls expose surface roughness, depth strength, depth scale, depth falloff, and clear distance. The response is `strength × scale × (max(0, distance − clearDistance) / scale)^falloff`. **Physical depth** sets strength/scale/falloff to one and clear distance to zero, recovering physical ray propagation. **Reference depth** exaggerates distance growth: nearby leaves retain shapes while distant foliage loses detail over a much broader footprint. This artistic remapping is separate from the interface model and absorption.

Color grading is a separate final pass shared by the glass and clear views. White balance precedes ACES tone mapping; a smooth perceptual contrast curve, black/white points, luminance-based saturation, vignette, and stable film grain follow. The contrast curve keeps its middle-gray pivot while retaining shadow and highlight gradation. **Reference grade** starts with lower saturation, deeper contrast, and slightly cooler color; **Neutral grade** resets these artistic adjustments. Exposure remains independently adjustable.

This remains a real-time raster/screen-space model. It uses finite path samples and filtered footprints, three-band conductor data, Schlick/GGX conductor lighting, and one prefiltered internal reflection; it does not solve full spectral transport, all internal bounces, or photon caustics. Rays beyond the expanded source buffer use its edge data. These implementation limits and the reference's unmeasured material properties prevent claiming an exact physical reconstruction.

The greenhouse has 20 deterministic plant clusters using four curved leaf shapes: elliptical, lanceolate, broader juvenile leaves, and fern leaflets. Sparse upper-left sprays grow from stems continuing below the view, while the right-hand shrubs carry most of the canopy. Yellowing leaves concentrate in a lower-left shoot. Irregular internodes, decussate pairs, spiral phyllotaxis, curved lateral shoots, tapered stems, varying leaf roll, and drooping leaves avoid repeated flat ladders. Petioles join the stems; color, blade twist, wax roughness, and thin-leaf backlighting vary with age. Leaves use procedural vein, pore, color, and normal maps. Shoots near the glass bend back smoothly, preserving shared branch endpoints. Swept blade bounds and branch strain limits keep the moving foliage behind the pane. Directional sunlight and restrained sky fill illuminate the leaves against a deep shaded recess.

`src/glass/dynamics.ts` advances damped spring modes at a fixed 120 Hz. Cantilever tip stiffness follows `3EI/L³`, with a circular stem's second moment; mode mass includes stem and leaf mass. Quadratic air drag loads each plant, and inertial coupling passes branch acceleration into leaf petiole pitch. Individual blades have area-dependent mass/inertia, pitch and torsion springs, damping, relative-airflow drag, and nonlinear stiffening. Larger leaves respond more slowly; fern leaflets react faster. Pressure changes with blade orientation. Calm air lets motion settle naturally, and pausing freezes the state. Elastic moduli, areal mass, and drag/damping coefficients are modeling assumptions rather than species measurements. This is a reduced-mode botanical motion model, not a full tissue or fluid simulation. Shared GPU deformation preserves attachment points and shadows; cofactor/Jacobian normal transforms keep the moving leaf and stem lighting consistent with their geometry.

Camera drift and pointer parallax are restrained. Click and drag to orbit within about 17° horizontally and 9° vertically, with gentle damping; orbit also works while animation is paused. The camera stays in front of the pane. Settings → **Match reference** restores the reference settings and original camera. **Space** or **Pause** stops ambient movement; reduced motion starts still. Hidden tabs stop drawing and suspend sound. Settings group diffusion, glass surface and wire mesh, optional embossing, light/shadow, color/tone, and movement/sound. Optical and grading controls save locally across refreshes; new controls merge their defaults into existing tuning. Versioned construction and reference-look updates apply once, including during hot refresh. The steel/softer-glass revision changes strand gauge/finish and front reflection while preserving the previous pass's custom grading, diffusion, camera, and pane settings. Subsequent adjustments persist normally. Rendering caps pixel density and the output buffers at 2.4 million pixels, and reduces resolution if startup frame times are consistently slow. GPU, texture, shadow, audio, and listener resources are released when leaving the page.

Choose **Rain ambience** under **Debug → Rain shower** to include tin-roof ambience or use touch audio alone. The tin-roof loop is by digifishmusic (CC BY 4.0). ## San Francisco breathing experiment

`/city/` is a separate, lazy-loaded Three.js study, available as **City** in the navigation. The simulation fills the page behind the site navigation, with one Settings button and the panel closed by default. There is no title, subtitle, footer readout, FPS counter or explanatory copy over the scene. Loading uses a small progress bar; actionable errors remain visible. The chess homepage retains its behavior. Both scenes read the same material and lighting constants from `src/chess/render/appearance.ts`: white/charcoal standard materials, roughness 0.35, metalness 0.04/0.06, ambient 0.12, hemisphere 0.4 and directional intensity 3.1. The city scales the light position and shadow bounds to cover its larger scene while preserving light direction and material response.

The full geometry contains 176,899 valid DataSF buildings. The default study is a compact Market Street crop: 256 real buildings, with no coastline. Its footprint bounds span about 780 × 825 metres. Downtown (5,592 buildings) and the full city remain available in the geometry selector. Camera presets fit the selected study. Source geometry, measured median building heights and minimum ground elevations come from [DataSF Building Footprints](https://data.sf.gov/d/ynuv-fyni); the survey dates to 2010 with later footprint refinements. [DPW shoreline geometry](https://services.arcgis.com/Zs2aNLFN00jrS4gG/ArcGIS/rest/services/CPAs_WFL1/FeatureServer/7) comes from a public ArcGIS mirror. Dataset attribution is recorded here and in the bundled manifest. This historic snapshot does not include modern landmark replacements.

Direct flow defaults to local matching on all three datasets. A Web Worker constructs only nearby tile/building candidates and runs a sparse auction, approximately minimizing squared travel without forcing a complete assignment. Every moving pair obeys a hard centre-distance limit (default two current tile widths). Every matched tile becomes exactly one building, and each building can use at most one tile. Unused tiles shrink at their original position. Strict mapping is the default: buildings without an assigned tile remain hidden throughout, including in the shadow pass. The optional Grow in place setting restores the previous fallback, interpolating unmatched buildings from a point at their own centre to preserve the complete survey at the city endpoint. A single instanced draw renders unused tiles, so the flat endpoint still covers the entire board. Perimeter origins align square corners with footprint edges to reduce twisting.

Grid tiles per building sets the total grid density from ¼× to 8×, rounded up to a square board over the same area, independently of the stored geometry. It does not allow a tile to split or a building to consume multiple tiles. The default is 4×: Market Street uses a 32 × 32 board with all 256 buildings mapped and 768 spare tiles. At the default travel limit, full-city coverage is 99.6% at 4× (176,186 buildings on 842 × 842 tiles), and 100% at 8× (176,899 buildings on 1,190 × 1,190 tiles). Density and travel-limit changes recompute matching off the render thread; the UI reports grid dimensions, mapped coverage, hidden/growing counts and travel. At 1× the full-city moving matches average about 16 m, with a maximum of 67 m; the prior forced assignment averaged 2.7 km and reached 5.7 km. The retained forced comparison uses the fixed baked Market 16 × 16 minimum-cost bijection or the older downtown 75 × 75 / full-city 421 × 421 parcel ordering. Those assignments also support the staged modes.

Simultaneous flow is the default: every vertex interpolates directly from the source square to its final survey footprint using the morph progress. There is no miniature fitted footprint or late expansion/release. Height grows throughout as progress raised to the adjustable rise-easing exponent (default 1.8), with overall breathing eased at both endpoints. Direct flow does not enforce collision constraints; irregular neighbours can intersect in transit. Earlier staged parcel weave and radial breath modes remain for comparison, with an optional parcel guard that does not affect direct flow. Transient roof triangles preserve complete source squares where the destination triangulator omits collinear edges; they collapse continuously at the city endpoint.

Controls include scrubbing, breathing/pause, tile matching, grid tiles per building, unmatched-building behavior, local travel limit, endpoint dwell, building height, rise easing, terrain scale, shared swell, ground, shadows, wireframe, slow orbit, dataset selection and camera presets. The earlier staged modes also expose their parcel guard and temporary tile gap; radial breath exposes wave spread. Space toggles breathing; R resets. Reduced motion starts paused. Hidden tabs stop rendering. The full city is about 43 MB compressed and 5.1 million building triangles; the default Market Street crop is about 71 KB compressed and 8,273 building triangles. The adjustable grid adds two instanced triangles per cell. Building heights default to measured scale (1×). Hill relief and shared swell default to zero, with the coast/terrain layer hidden, so building bases remain on one level plane throughout the morph. Hill relief and swell can be enabled independently. Roofs are flat extrusions, courtyards are filled, edges simplify at 0.8 m, and terrain is interpolated from building ground measurements with approximate park relief.

To rebuild public geometry, download the DataSF resource with fields `sf16_bldgid,shape,gnd_min_m,hgt_median_m,peak_1st_m` and the shoreline GeoJSON, keep raw downloads outside this checkout, then run:

```sh
node --max-old-space-size=4096 scripts/prepare-city.mjs /path/to/buildings.json /path/to/shoreline.geojson
```

To rebuild only the Market Street crop while retaining the existing full-city and downtown binaries, append `--market-only`.

The compact binaries use quantized positions (about 0.22 m precision), normalized perimeter coordinates, indexed roof/wall triangles, and a floating-point per-building data texture. DecompressionStream inflates the bundled gzip files in current browsers. All animation runs in vertex shaders, including the matching shadow deformation. `npm run check` checks endpoint timing, continuous direct-flow height, guarded containment in the staged modes and source-square coverage in addition to existing chess tests. Local-matching tests exercise contested tiles, distant fallback, uniqueness, all grid-density presets, preservation of the survey extent, starting-grid coverage and the hard travel limit against the real datasets. Shader checks cover strict suppression in both the building and shadow programs. The asset verifier checks every baked source square and indexed geometry, including the Market forced-assignment cost. Browser and visual verification require an explicit request under `../AGENTS.md`.

City shader regression tests expand Three.js shader chunks, preprocess conditional GLSL, and parse the generated building, shadow and unused-tile vertex/fragment shaders. They cover the reserved-identifier failure and the unsigned integer input required by Three's Uint32 vertex attribute binding in the building and shadow programs. These are nonvisual checks, not GPU verification. The renderer reports shader compilation failures in the preview UI as well as the console.
