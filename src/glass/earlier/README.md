# Earlier glass study

This is the independent rendering fork immediately before the user's request to aggressively match the reference image. The scene files were uncommitted during that work; this implementation was restored from the source and edits recorded in the conversation. Historical browser-only slider values were not archived when the fork was created.

As of 2026-11-07, the public `/glass/` page uses **Original** with the locked `earlier:original` source preset in [`../default-presets.json`](../default-presets.json), without site navigation, comparison controls, toolbar, or thumbnail. Old version/arrangement URLs also open this presentation. Its orbit limits expand to ±.48 rad yaw / ±.26 rad pitch, with exponential slowing near both ends, smooth inertia, immediate reversal, and wheel/pinch zoom. Ambience activates on the first interaction and follows tab visibility.

The bottom-left **Debug** button toggles the Original tuning panel using React state. **Save page defaults** writes the live settings into the project JSON on localhost; **Copy JSON**, **Export JSON**, and the `glass-settings-snapshot` textarea provide an exact recovery snapshot. Public debug edits remain live until saved and do not replace workbench draft slots. Static-site debug saves persist personally in that browser and explicitly report their scope. The clean presentation reads source defaults independently of legacy study drafts/reset defaults. The complete comparison UI is available locally inside **Archived scenes → Compare previous scenes**. Menu and scene choices never enter the URL.

On 2026-11-01, Neilson's subsequent tuning was captured from the open Earlier tab. The complete settings and audio state are backed up in [`presets/neilson-2026-11-01.json`](presets/neilson-2026-11-01.json). Active controls were read from their exact DOM value attributes and checkbox state; renderer metadata and the three dormant lighting fields retain their source values. That historical factory fallback has front micro-roughness .205, depth strength 1.65, depth scale 1.44 m, depth falloff 1, exposure .4, contrast 1.8, black point .12, and white point .85. The newer source preset overrides this fallback. Existing local workbench adjustments still load normally; the Current version is independent.

The **Original** arrangement uses 23 plantings and 989 leaves across two shapes, procedural sway, original leaf maps and 8-bit glass normal/height maps, 256px broad-window HDR capture, pale plaster and pots, .76 mm zinc-like embedded mesh, and the earlier linear contrast grade. Each blade uses 77 vertices to shape a tapered silhouette, curled tip, and raised midrib. Six-sided branches follow the attachment nodes, and four-sided leaf stalks retain their bend. It includes the wired-glass correction: the metal mesh is enabled and the optional raised glass lattice is zero.

**Close canopy** now uses seven authored plantings: three open sprays on the left and four overlapping plush shoots on the right. Irregular internodes, curved branches, tapered stems, varied leaf roll, drooping blades, and three leaf silhouettes break up the outline. Both clusters include near and farther foliage; there is no distant foliage blanket. Enlarged warm-white plaster patches remain behind the sparse left branches, with a wider dark interruption and an open black middle. Deep cool greens, olive/new growth, varied wax roughness, and occasional yellow leaves add contrast without replacing the user's saved grade or optical settings.

Both sides use procedural breeze, with no active spring simulation. Rooted branch bending combines slow directional gusts and crosswind; hanging shoots use their signed stem height. Individual leaf phases add pitch, twist, and a softer, faster tip flex. Leaf bases remain attached to their petioles. **Breeze** ranges from zero to **500%**; higher values increase branch travel and gust speed while leaf flex saturates smoothly. Zero is exactly still, and pause freezes the common animation time. Blade and branch cofactor normal transforms track the deformed surfaces, so specular highlights follow the motion. Beauty and shadow programs share the same deformation. Forward shoots reduce depth travel and blade rotation; conservative swept bounds retain at least 2 cm front-face clearance, while farther blades keep a 10 cm envelope. Leaves keep their curved shape rather than flattening against the pane.

**Close canopy · v1** preserves the first, pressed-leaf canopy with 37 plantings and 1,591 leaves. Only that arrangement uses smooth contact deformation at z = −.02 m. **Close canopy · v2** preserves the full 1,591-leaf layout, pulled back from the glass, with the first left/right motion comparison and larger pale patches. **Close canopy · v3** preserves the pruned finite-pane layout before the forward shoots. **Close canopy · v4** preserves the previous six-shoot composition, with a near shoot on each side, stronger Current spring input on the left, and original sway on the right. Archived comparisons retain their 150% Breeze maximum. Latest placement and breeze changes do not alter these entries; finite-pane grid alignment fixes apply to all finite entries.

The **Arrangement** selector switches layouts on the same page while carrying camera orbit and scroll-wheel zoom. Optical controls and reset defaults are independent per arrangement. A new layout initially inherits the existing Original tuning; archived canopy entries start from existing canopy tuning. Subsequent edits persist independently. Add a configuration entry in `arrangements.ts` to introduce another composition: plantings, pale wall patches, background, lighting, motion, pruning, and optional pane bounds. The selector, settings slots, bookmark URLs, and defaults-saving endpoint derive their names from that map. Preserve previous entries when iterating.

Rendering, optics, textures, environment, original planting, grading, and random/noise modules remain in this folder. Original and v1 retain their earlier rendering behavior. The split-motion arrangements deliberately import Current's foliage/dynamics implementation for the left-hand comparison, passing Earlier's leaf geometry, textures, and material appearance. The common settings type and camera-view type, UI, and audio are shared by the page.

Use the local debug panel's archived-scene selector for Close canopy and its previous layouts. Choices use React state; reload returns to the locked Original. Workbench Original settings retain `neilsonks:glass:earlier:v1`; other arrangements use `neilsonks:glass:earlier:<arrangement>:v1`, independent of Current. Normalization never applies current reference or steel migrations.

**Close canopy · v3** preserves the previous finite composition. The finite material shares its grid origin and strand-warp uniform directly with the wire generator. This keeps optional glass crests aligned when pane centering, grid pitch, or wire gauge changes. Infinite archived entries preserve their original crest phase.

**Save as defaults** writes the selected layout's optical/grading/movement settings to `../default-presets.json` through the local Vite server and updates its reset defaults. These source presets ship in future builds. On a published static site, the button saves personal defaults in that browser and reports that scope. Slider autosave is separate, so experiments after a save leave the reset defaults intact. The archived capture remains unchanged.

**Glass bump size** rescales the period of both height/normal maps and applies the inverse scale to their slopes, keeping reflected and transmitted rays consistent with the height field. Bump strength changes height independently. **Wire thickness** changes cylindrical mesh diameter from .4 to 1.8 mm.

A single instanced mesh draws both wire directions, with one opaque quad per strand. The fragment shader supplies the round metal normal and subpixel coverage. The light-facing quads also cast wire shadows. The glass shader resolves the lit metal on a single refracted path per pixel, with depth correction and filtered coverage on thin wires. Its 24 scattering samples are reserved for the view through the pane. The front shader still models light exiting the rough rear interface; this does not require a second mesh. When the pane is wet, the shader reads the bead and film surface prepared by the water pass.

Click the FPS counter to change GPU quality without restarting the scene. Start with glass scattering (1–48 rays, default 24), then garden depth and front relief refinements (both default to 3). Wire antialiasing and glass texture filtering offer the levels supported by the device. Water map size changes shading detail; airborne rain changes the number of actual falling drops and their collisions. Quality choices are local to the browser and independent of saved artwork presets. **Reset GPU quality** restores the original budgets.

**Pixel ratio** defaults to the display’s native density. A fixed **1×** draws one pixel per CSS pixel on each axis. **Render size** shows the actual canvas buffer dimensions. Compare **Render GPU** at the same ratio in three views: the complete scene, **Disable glass pane** for the lit garden, and **Mesh only** for the same garden geometry colored by its surface normals. Mesh only draws directly to the canvas and skips shadows, rain drawing, and grading. The simulation continues across view changes. The comparison checkboxes are temporary and independent of the saved scene.

The latest canopy and v3/v4 use a finite 3.3 × 3.5 m sheet with one front-facing surface, transparent cut edges, and embedded wire ribbons. Their cameras can orbit freely. From behind, the garden and cut edges remain visible, and the renderer skips the refraction targets. Current and the older arrangements retain their restrained camera limits.

Nonvisual checks cover the restored planting count, texture format, defaults, storage isolation, and expanded GLSL. Visual verification requires an explicit user request under the repository's `AGENTS.md`.
